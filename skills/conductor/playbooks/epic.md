### Epic

**You own the ticket graph and the epic's verdict.** Lanes own their tickets, through to the merge. You decide what runs, in what order, and when the epic is done. You run no checks and no merges yourself, because every turn of yours re-reads the whole epic.

Several tickets toward one outcome, each ending in its own PR. You dispatch one lane per ticket and steer them. You write no ticket's code. A single ticket is not an epic, so run its own playbook.

1. **Pin the tickets.** The tracker holds the epic and one issue per ticket. When the operator gave you a body of work rather than tickets, cut it into tickets and create the issues yourself. With no tracker, write the tickets to one file outside the repo's tree and paste each ticket in full into its lane brief. Each ticket must stand alone: the outcome, the files it owns, what is out of scope, and acceptance that ends in an end-to-end check. Fill the gaps yourself from the code. Product and preference calls go to the operator through ask, batched into one round. Settle in the ticket each design point the code can answer, such as where a boundary sits, what a key holds or which reads a change covers. A new data shape or a crossed module boundary is a point to settle, not a flag by itself. Flag a ticket **needs design** only when a point depends on what the lane will find, such as a cut chosen from a measurement, or when you could not make its acceptance concrete. Its lane runs at frontier. Every other lane runs at strong-code, because its ticket arrives specified and its reviewer still runs at frontier. Name each ticket's end-to-end check concretely enough, a spec and the screenshots it must show, that its verifier can run at strong-code, per roles.md Tiers.
2. **Draw the graph.** For each ticket, list the files it writes and the tickets it needs. show it as a Mermaid diagram. Tickets that others need, such as a schema change, a migration, shared types or a flag, are the backbone. Two tickets whose write lists overlap get split or chained, per [separate-before-sharing](../principles/separate-before-sharing.md). Record the graph on the epic's issue, or in the tickets file.
3. **Pace the dispatches by the queue.** Every ready ticket gets a lane, and the box queue sets the pace. Every lane runs heavy checks through it, per [queue-heavy-checks](../principles/queue-heavy-checks.md), and a lane parked on a full queue still costs: after a long wait, its next turn pays full price to read its whole context again. So before each dispatch, run the queue's `status`. While a queue your lanes use holds a waiter and one of your own lanes is still working, hold the next dispatch: that lane's next notice runs this check again. With none of your lanes working, dispatch anyway, because no notice would come to release the hold. The first wave goes out together, since fresh lanes have queued nothing yet. A lane whose PR is merge-ready and waiting to land runs no checks, so it never holds a dispatch.
4. **Dispatch the ready tickets.** Find the repo's documented command that prepares a fresh worktree once, and put it in every brief's SETUP. Name in CONTEXT the sections of any other skill the ticket needs, such as one design command's reference, and never tell a lane to load a whole skill. A ticket is ready when every ticket it needs has merged. It is also ready when all but one have merged and that one has a merge-ready PR, and then its lane stacks on that PR's branch. A ticket waiting on two unmerged tickets waits for the merges. When ready work waits only on merges, ask the operator once to land those PRs. dispatch one lane per ready ticket, at the pace from step 3, each with a lane brief from roles.md. track each ticket as its own item, led by its number, and update it from the lanes' reports. Every lane you dispatch, here or in step 5, gets a lane watch in the background, as the binding says, because a lane that stalls mid-turn sends no report. Stopping a lane always stops its watch too, or the stopped lane reads as stalled.
5. **Steer by notices.** Each lane reports back when it is merge-ready, its head moved, it is blocked or it failed. wait for those reports and never poll. A notice that needs nothing from the operator gets no reply text: update the ticket's item and end the turn. Write to the operator only when a ticket lands or fails, a lane needs a product call, or the operator asked. After every notice, dispatch what step 3 held, per step 3. When a notice arrives:
   - **Merge-ready.** Read the reviewer's STATUS and the verifier's verdict on the PR, and the diff's file list and size. A lane saying "ready" is a claim. spawn a reviewer only when the files or the size surprise you. A lane under LAND waits for your go before it merges, so send go, through continue, once this check passes. Then dispatch the next ready ticket, per step 3.
   - **Context full.** A lane that compacts in place, where the binding says it does, rarely sends this. When any lane does, dispatch the same brief at the same tier, with BRANCH set to the pushed branch and the report pasted in full under CONTEXT. stop the old lane. This is a handover, not an escalation.
   - **Cut off by a usage limit.** A lane whose notice says the account hit its usage limit lost nothing but its turn: its context, worktree and pushed branch are intact. After the reset the notice names, continue every cut-off lane with one line that says to go on from where it stopped. Never dispatch a fresh lane for it, before the reset or after, because a fresh lane redoes the work a continue keeps for the price of one cache reload. A continued lane already past its context limit pushes and reports Context full, and that bullet takes over.
   - **Head moved.** Lanes stacked on that branch see it through their own watcher and rebase themselves. Message a stacked lane only when it is not watching, such as a lane you just resumed.
   - **Merged.** Close the lane per step 8, then dispatch the next ready ticket, per step 3. A lane that landed under LAND reports its own post-merge check, so you run none.
   - **Escalate.** A Failed report, or a Blocked report on a design question from a lane at strong-code, goes one tier up, per roles.md Escalation. have the lane push its branch, stop it, and dispatch the same brief at frontier with BRANCH set to that branch and the failure attached. A failure at frontier comes back to you.
   - **Blocked.** Any other block gets a reply. Settle what you can. Product calls go to the operator through ask. A second block on the same premise means [attack-the-premise](../principles/attack-the-premise.md) before anything else.
   - **A change others build on.** When one lane changes an interface another lane uses, tell that lane what changed and where.
   - **Stalled.** The lane watch reports a lane quiet in the middle of a turn. Read the lane's state, as the binding says, then continue it with what it was doing and what its worktree holds, and start its watch again. A second stall in the same stage means stop it and dispatch the same brief with BRANCH set to its pushed branch, as for Context full.
   - **Box slow.** The watch reports several lanes quiet at once, or a stall arrives with the box's load above its cores. The box is the cause, not the lanes. Check the queue, start the watch again and wait. Replace no lane while the box is overloaded, because a fresh lane adds load. stop a lane only to replace it, and say why on its ticket.
   - **Watch ended.** A watch that times out or fails starts again. A watch that reports its lane gone is stopped, and the lane's state decides the rest.
6. **Verify the epic.** When the operator asked to land each ticket as it passes, run this once on trunk after the last merge, and spawn the verifier there. It can no longer stop a merge, so a failure becomes a new ticket. Otherwise, when every ticket's PR is merge-ready, build the combined result in a scratch worktree, the top of the stack or every branch merged in graph order, yourself or through an editor, and run SETUP there. Then spawn one verifier on that worktree against the epic's own acceptance. One ticket can pass alone while the epic still fails, so this check is never skipped.
7. **Land on request.** Only an explicit request to land, ship or merge lets a ticket land. Pass it to each lane it covers as LAND, quoted, through continue, and to later lanes in their brief. Each lane lands its own PR per roles.md "Lanes", in graph order because a stacked lane lands only once its base has merged and it has rebased onto trunk. Each lane owns its own rebase. A request such as "merge them as soon as they pass" covers every lane until the operator withdraws it. Pass a withdrawal to each lane as `LAND no`.
8. **Close the lanes.** When a ticket's PR has merged, stop its lane and its watch, clean worktrees and tick its item. Update the epic's issue, or the tickets file, with what landed, what did not, and why.

9. **Hand over the epic.** When the binding tells you that your context passed the limit, finish the notice in hand and start nothing new. A small context steers cheaper, and this one re-reads a large one on every notice.
   - Write the epic's state to its issue, or the tickets file: one row per ticket with its lane's agent id and task id, its branch, PR and status, the operator's standing instructions quoted, such as a LAND request, and the next step.
   - When the binding can **hand over in place**, do that as the last call of your turn, and end the turn. The session shrinks to what you keep and goes on by itself. Its lanes and watches keep running, so nothing is stopped or re-dispatched, and the operator does nothing.
   - Otherwise, ask every working lane to push its branch and report Context full, through continue, wait for those reports, and add each in full to the issue. Then stop the lanes and their watches and tell the operator in one line to start a fresh session that continues the epic from that issue. The fresh session re-dispatches each open ticket's brief with BRANCH set to its pushed branch, its report under CONTEXT, and REPORT TO set to its own name for lanes run as separate sessions.

**Reply:** one row per ticket with its PR as a full URL, its verdict and whether it landed, the graph as it ended, the epic verifier's verdict, and what is left for the operator, in the order and form of SKILL.md "Writing the reply".
